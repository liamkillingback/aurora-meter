## The guarantee in one paragraph

Aurora Meter does not promise that a scheduled job runs once. A cron plugin can
tick twice across a leader change, a rescued job runs again, two nodes can both
be told to sweep, and a job that finished can die before Oban records it. What
each operation promises instead is that the **second** run finds the work done
and says so: expiry re-reads `expired_at` under the grant row's own
`FOR UPDATE`, the hold reconciler re-reads `status = 'pending'` under the hold
row's, the reporter's staging collapses onto the outbox identity tuple, and the
outbox deliverer's every transition carries a fencing token. Oban's `unique`
option is defence in depth: it saves the duplicate run's work and it is not what
keeps the ledger right.

> **Invariant I16.** Worker scheduling is not an exactly-once assumption.

Owner: build unit **05c**. Contributors: 04b (the outbox lease token), 06d (the
recurrence key), 03d (the replay checkpoint).

One row per worker, in both packages, for the four cases the invariant map names:
**duplicate jobs**, **two nodes**, **kill and resume**, and **a stale write**.
`docs/correctness.md` in each package names every test listed below.

## Does Oban's `unique` stop a duplicate job? Three measurements, and they disagree

This is the question `real-provider-verification.md` gives phase 05, and the
answer depends on how close together the two inserts are. All three numbers
below are measured; the first draft of this file reported only the middle one
and drew a conclusion the other two do not support.

### 1. Sequential, one connection: uniqueness holds

`RestartTest` / `I16 two sequential AutoTopUpSweeper runs enqueue exactly one
job per tenant`. The first sweep enqueues 6, the second enqueues **0**, and every
tenant has exactly one job. Oban's `unique` refuses the second insert.

### 2. Two BEAM nodes, released together but separated by an `:rpc` hop: one row

`05c-multinode.log`, five runs, two real BEAM nodes (node A under `mix run`,
node B a plain `erl` sharing node A's code paths) against one Postgres database,
the shape 02c and 05b used.

| | Run 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rows after two nodes insert the same **unique** `CreditExpiry` job | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| Rows after the same insert with **`unique` removed** (the X125 control) | **2** | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Expiry rows after two nodes **execute** over 24 due grants | 24 | 24 | 24 | 24 | 24 |
| Rows after two nodes insert the same `AutoTopUpSweeper` job | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 | 1 |

The control discriminates: one row with uniqueness, two without it, nothing else
changed. So uniqueness **is** what produced the single row here.

**But the two inserts were not simultaneous, and the harness cannot make them
so.** The barrier releases both sides at once; node A then inserts in process
while node B's insert travels over an `:rpc` hop first. Node A wins every time,
which is also why the execution split is identical in all five runs. What this
measurement establishes is that uniqueness deduplicates two inserts separated by
a sub-millisecond gap. It does not establish that it deduplicates two inserts
that arrive together, and the next measurement shows it does not.

### 3. Two independent connections, one VM, forced barrier: uniqueness loses, 10 of 10

`RestartTest` / `I16 a forced race on one tenant shows how often Oban's unique
loses it`. Both inserters are spawned, both report ready, and only then are both
released, so the two `Oban.insert/1` calls really are concurrent.

```
[05c race] %{test: :forced_insert_race, rows_per_race: [2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2]}
[05c race] %{of: 10, test: :forced_insert_race, contended: 10}
```

**Two rows, every time.** And the same thing at sweep scale: two concurrent
`AutoTopUpSweeper.sweep/0` runs on independent connections gave every one of six
tenants two jobs (`%{enqueued: [6, 6], per_tenant: [2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2]}`).

### What that means, and why it is not a defect

**Oban's `unique` is a check inside the insert, not a constraint on the table.**
Two inserts on two connections can both pass it, and the loser is not refused:
it is simply not told. So on a busy installation a tenant can have two jobs for
one intent, and `open-findings.md` X209 records the measurement.

It costs nothing here, and saying why is the whole of I16. Every worker's
**effect** is guarded by something that is not a job count:

- `AutoTopUpWorker` takes `AuroraMeter.Pro.Credits.with_lock/2` and then finds
  the pending idempotency key already set, so the second job **resumes the same
  attempt** rather than starting a second one, and Stripe answers one
  PaymentIntent under one key.
- `CreditExpiry` re-reads `expired_at` under the grant row's `FOR UPDATE`.
- The outbox deliverer claims with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` and fences every
  transition with a lease token.

That is the invariant stated exactly: **scheduling is not exactly-once, and the
effect is.** A reader who took "uniqueness prevents the duplicate" from the
middle measurement alone would have believed the wrong layer was protecting the
card.

### The contended branch, and how often it ran

Executing `CreditExpiry` on both nodes over 24 due grants:

| | node A | node B |
|---|---|---|
| examined | 24 | 23 |
| expired | 22 | 2 |
| skipped (**the contended branch**) | 2 | 21 |

23 executions of the `:already_expired` branch per run, five runs, and
`22 + 2 = 24` effects for 24 grants. Compare X187, where an unassisted
two-connection race gave that branch **zero** executions over ten seeds. Node B
examined 23 rather than 24 because the two share one cursor, which is the design
(L05c-1) rather than a defect.

## G05 bullet 4: a restart and a reconnect erase nothing

`RestartTest` / `G05 a queue restart and a database reconnect leave every
in-flight payload byte identical`.

An outbox item and a credit account with a pending attempt are staged and
committed. Then, in order: the Oban supervisor child is terminated (asserted
through `Supervisor.which_children/1`, which is the only one of three spellings
that actually answers the question); the connection carrying the work is
terminated **at the server** with `pg_terminate_backend(pg_backend_pid())`, which
raises on the statement that asked for it; the pool is proved to have replaced
it by a statement that succeeds; and the Oban child is restarted.

Four values are then compared, **as bytes read back out of Postgres with
`::text`** rather than as decoded terms, because a `jsonb` map decoded and
re-encoded compares equal as a term while the stored bytes may differ:

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `aurora_meter_outbox_items.payload::text` | identical | identical |
| `payload ->> 'identifier'` | identical | identical |
| `aurora_meter_credit_accounts.pending_params::text` | identical | identical |
| `pending_idempotency_key` | identical | identical |

and the item is still `pending`, so nothing moved it to a terminal state on the
way past. All four are asserted non-`nil` first, so "byte identical" is not two
absences agreeing with each other.

Two things this test found about the harness rather than about the system, both
recorded as findings: Oban cannot be restarted while the sandbox is in `:manual`
mode, because `verify_migrated!/1` runs from the supervisor's own process and
reads a refused query as a missing table (X210); and terminating **every**
backend on the database rather than one leaves the pool with nothing working at
the instant Oban restarts, and leaks a dead socket into the next test (X210 as
well).

## Where I16 has no second layer, and this unit is saying so

- **The checkpoint write is not in the batch's transaction.** A crash between a
  batch's commit and its cursor write re-runs that batch. Nothing in the database
  prevents that, and nothing should: the alternative enlists an operational row
  in a money transaction. It is safe only because every operation using the loop
  re-reads under a row lock, and a later operation whose effects are not
  idempotent **cannot use this loop**. That sentence is in
  `AuroraMeter.Operations`' moduledoc as well as here.
- **`AuroraMeter.Pro.Rollup`'s month aggregate is unbounded.** It is unique and
  pausable and has no cursor. A second node running it concurrently produces the
  same upserts, so the effect is right; the run time is not bounded.
- **X213 is new, and it is the most consequential thing this unit found.**
  `Promotions.consume/3` raises `KeyError` when an `:expire` entry sorts before
  its own grant, which the ledger's node-stamped `inserted_at` makes reachable
  whenever the wall clock steps backwards mid-run. It was caught because 05c's
  fifty-grant resume test is the first thing in either package to write fifty
  expiry rows in one run: one full-suite run in ten came back with 43 expiries
  instead of 50. The per-item rescue this unit added (X200) is what turned a
  crash into a **silent shortfall**, so the test now asserts the total after
  draining and a deterministic reproduction sits beside it. 06a's `seq` is the
  fix.
- **X183 is still open** and is the strongest statement of where I11 and I16
  meet: `aurora_meter_credit_transactions` has no check constraint that could
  refuse a `:release` beside a `:settle` for one hold, so the hold row lock is
  the only layer. 06a owns the remedy.

## Why this file has one name

`invariant-map.md` requires every invariant to resolve to exactly one evidence
file named `i<nn>.md`, and both packages' `docs/correctness.md` already pointed
here. 05c wrote the index under a second name as well, and the two halves then
disagreed: this file cited a Pro `05c-i16.md` that was never written
(`open-findings.md` X202). Merged at the 05c review, and the rule is the map's
one: **one invariant, one file, named for the invariant.**
